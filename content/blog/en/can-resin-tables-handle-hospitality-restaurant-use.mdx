We get asked this a lot, and the honest answer has conditions attached.

Yes, resin tables can work in hospitality. They are not the low-maintenance option, and there are settings where we would tell you to buy something else.

## What commercial use actually involves

A private dining table might see two hours of use a day, gentle handling, and mild cleaning.

A restaurant table sees continuous covers, plates arriving hot, glasses and cutlery dragged across it, staff clearing at speed, and commercial cleaning products applied many times daily by people whose priority is turnaround rather than surface preservation.

That is a different order of exposure, and specification has to reflect it.

## The three real risks

### 1. Heat

The most serious, by a wide margin.

Epoxy is a thermoset that softens at temperatures well below a plate coming off a pass. Because the resin is structural rather than a coating, the damage is a permanent depression, not a surface mark. It cannot be polished out.

In a restaurant, hot plates land on tables constantly. This is not a specification problem — it is an operational one. It requires either placemats, or service standards that guarantee a plate never goes directly onto resin, or accepting the damage.

Of everything on this page, this is the one that most often decides whether a resin table is appropriate.

### 2. Cleaning chemicals

Commercial sanitisers, degreasers, and alcohol-based products are considerably harsher than domestic cleaners, and they are applied repeatedly.

Over time they dull finishes and can attack the wood-resin interface. Staff cannot be relied upon to use a specific product indefinitely — turnover is high and the bottle nearest the station is what gets used.

A film finish tolerates this far better than an oil.

### 3. Abrasion

Cutlery, glassware bases, and plates dragged rather than lifted. Resin scratches more readily than glass, and a surface that accumulates fine scratching loses its clarity over a few years.

## What we change for commercial specification

**Finish:** polyurethane, not hard-wax oil. The added resistance to spills, chemicals, and abrasion is worth the loss of local repairability. [The full comparison](/blog/epoxy-vs-polyurethane-table-finish) explains the reasoning.

**Resin treatment:** we lean toward tinted or opaque pours rather than crystal-clear. A clear pour shows every scratch and every bit of ambering; a tinted one is considerably more forgiving. [Resin colours and finishes](/blog/resin-customization-colors-and-finishes) covers the options.

**Dimensions:** thinner tops, less overhang, more conservative proportions. Commercial furniture gets leaned on, sat on, and moved.

**Base:** heavier and more rigid. Restaurant tables are repositioned constantly.

## What we tell hospitality clients honestly

**Expect visible wear.** A commercial resin table will not look new in year five. It will look used. If your concept requires a pristine surface indefinitely, this is the wrong material.

**Budget for refinishing.** A film finish will eventually need sanding back and recoating. Treat this as a maintenance line item, not a failure.

**Train the staff.** The single highest-return intervention is a rule about hot plates. It costs nothing and prevents the one form of damage that cannot be repaired.

**Order spares if the concept demands consistency.** If you need twenty matching tables and one is destroyed in year two, a replacement from a different tree will not match. Commission the spare at the outset — [matching sets](/blog/matching-sets-dining-coffee-side-tables) explains why matching cannot be done retrospectively.

## Where we would advise against

**High-volume casual dining.** Fast turnover, minimal service standards, and plates going straight onto tables. The economics do not work and neither does the material.

**Outdoor or covered-outdoor.** UV exposure ambers resin quickly, and humidity swings stress the wood-resin interface. We do not recommend it, and we will say so.

**Anywhere with direct sustained sunlight.** A conservatory or fully glazed dining room will age a resin surface visibly faster. [Care and placement](/blog/how-to-care-for-a-resin-river-table) covers the mechanism.

**Projects where the table must be replaceable.** These pieces are one-of-a-kind by nature. If your model requires identical replacements on demand, that is fundamentally at odds with the material.

## Where it works very well

**Fine dining**, where service standards are high, covers are fewer, and the table is part of the experience.

**Hotel bars and lounges**, where use is moderate and visual impact matters.

**Private dining rooms** within restaurants — lower volume, higher value, and the table is a feature.

**Chef's tables and counters**, where the piece is meant to be seen and the service is attentive.

**Reception and lobby furniture**, which is the strongest commercial fit of all — see [reception desks](/blog/custom-epoxy-reception-desks-for-offices).

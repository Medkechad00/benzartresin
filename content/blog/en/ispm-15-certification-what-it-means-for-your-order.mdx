ISPM 15 is not something most furniture buyers have heard of, and it becomes extremely relevant the moment a crate reaches a border.

## What it is

ISPM 15 is International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15, published under the International Plant Protection Convention. It regulates wood packaging material moving in international trade.

The concern is pests. Untreated timber can carry bark beetles, longhorn beetles, wood wasps, and nematodes. Wooden pallets and crates have historically been an effective vector for moving these across continents, and introduced wood pests have caused enormous damage to forests in regions with no natural resistance to them.

ISPM 15 exists to close that route.

## What it requires

Wood packaging must be treated by an approved method — typically heat treatment to a specified core temperature for a specified duration, or fumigation — and then marked.

The mark carries the IPPC symbol, a country code, a unique identifier for the facility that performed the treatment, and a code for the treatment method used. It must be permanent, legible, and visible on at least two opposite sides of the crate.

Crucially, it identifies the treating facility. The chain is traceable.

## What it means for your table

Every BenzArt crate is built from ISPM 15 compliant timber and marked accordingly.

The alternative is not a paperwork inconvenience. A non-compliant crate arriving in a country that enforces the standard — which is most of the world's major markets — can be:

- refused entry and returned at the shipper's cost
- ordered treated at the border, at your expense and on the border's timetable
- destroyed

Any of these outcomes puts a one-of-a-kind table at real risk, and none of them are worth saving on crating.

## What it does not cover

This is the point most often misunderstood.

**ISPM 15 governs packaging, not product.** Your finished table is not wood packaging material. It is a manufactured article, and it is not regulated under ISPM 15.

Some countries do have separate import rules concerning timber species and legal origin — regulations on illegally harvested timber, and restrictions on protected species under CITES. These are distinct from ISPM 15 and depend on your destination and the species involved.

If your commission involves an unusual species and you are importing into a jurisdiction with strict timber legislation, tell us at enquiry so we can confirm documentation before the piece is built rather than after.

## Why we mention it at all

Because it is a reasonable signal about how a workshop operates.

A studio that treats crating as an afterthought — that ships a valuable, irreplaceable piece in whatever timber was to hand — is telling you something about the rest of its process. Correct crating costs money and adds a step. It is also the last thing standing between months of bench work and a customs incinerator.

Our crates are built around the specific piece, from compliant marked timber, designed to be opened without power tools. [How shipping actually works](/blog/international-shipping-and-handling) covers the rest of the journey.

Nothing, mostly. Compliance is our responsibility as shipper.

Two things worth doing:

1. **Tell us your destination country early**, so we can confirm any additional requirements.
2. **Do not discard the crate immediately** if there is any chance of a damage claim. Photograph the markings along with everything else.

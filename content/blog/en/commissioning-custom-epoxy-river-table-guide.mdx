Commissioning a table is not the same as buying one. There is no stock to pick from, no size chart, and no photograph of the exact object that will arrive — because it does not exist yet. That is unfamiliar territory for most buyers, and the uncertainty is usually what stalls the decision rather than the price.

This guide sets out how the process actually runs, what drives the cost, and what you are agreeing to at each stage.

## What "made to order" actually means

Every BenzArt piece begins as a specific board from a specific tree. We do not hold finished inventory, and we do not produce a design more than once in the same way, because no two slabs are alike. The grain, the shape of the live edge, the voids that the resin fills — these are properties of the timber, not decisions we make.

The practical consequence is that you are commissioning a collaboration with a material that has its own opinions. A slab may reveal a check or an inclusion once it is surfaced. Good outcomes come from designing with those features rather than against them.

## The four stages

**1. Consultation.** You describe the space, the dimensions you need, seating capacity, and the look you are drawn to. We talk through wood species, resin treatment, and base design. Nothing is charged at this stage.

**2. Design and quote.** We source three to five candidate slabs matching your brief, photograph each in raw form, and issue a fixed quote against the board you choose. A 50% deposit secures the slab and your place in the queue.

**3. Crafting.** The slab is dried and moisture-tested, flattened, stabilised, and poured. Deep pours go in layers to control the exothermic reaction. After full cure the piece is flattened again, then sanded and finished by hand through progressive grits.

**4. Crating and shipping.** The finished table is crated in ISPM 15 certified timber and handed to a freight partner. The balance falls due before dispatch.

For a stage-by-stage breakdown with realistic durations, see [the full custom table timeline](/blog/from-consultation-to-shipping-timeline).

## What drives the price

Four variables account for most of the spread between a $3,000 table and a $15,000 one:

- **The slab.** Width, length, thickness, species, and figure. A single wide board that yields a 100cm top without joining is dramatically rarer — and more expensive — than two narrower boards.
- **Resin volume.** Resin is sold by weight and priced accordingly. A wide river in a thick top can require many litres, and deep pours need more curing time.
- **Finish complexity.** A clear pour on a flat top is straightforward. Pigment layering, suspended elements, and [sculpted or textured surfaces](/blog/sculpted-and-textured-resin-surfaces) add bench hours.
- **Base and destination.** Steel bases are fabricated to order, and freight for a heavy, fragile, crated object varies enormously by country.

[How our pricing is calculated](/blog/how-our-pricing-is-calculated) breaks each of these down with worked examples.

## Choosing materials

Wood species sets the entire character of the finished piece — colour, grain behaviour, hardness, and how it will age. [Walnut, olive, and maple](/blog/choosing-your-wood-walnut-vs-olive-vs-maple) each behave differently under resin and light.

Resin choice is the other half. Clear pours read as water and show everything beneath; opaque pigments read as stone and hide the underside of the live edge entirely. [Resin colours and finishes](/blog/resin-customization-colors-and-finishes) covers the palette and what each treatment does visually.

## Getting it to you

We ship globally. The two things worth understanding early are that all our crating timber carries ISPM 15 certification — the international phytosanitary standard that keeps wooden packaging from being refused or destroyed at the border — and that import duty is assessed by your country, not by us. [International shipping and handling](/blog/international-shipping-and-handling) explains what to expect on arrival.

## Before you enquire

Have three things roughly settled: the maximum footprint your room can take, the seating count you need, and a budget range. Those three constrain the slab search more than any aesthetic preference, and they let us tell you quickly whether what you want is achievable.

Everything else — species, colour, edge treatment, base — we can work through together.

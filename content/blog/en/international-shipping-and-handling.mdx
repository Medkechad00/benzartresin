A finished dining table is heavy, rigid, valuable, and irreplaceable. It cannot be remade if it is destroyed in transit, because the slab it came from no longer exists.

This is how we get one to you, and what you need to know as the person receiving it.

## Crating

Every piece travels in a crate built specifically for it. There is no standard box.

The table is wrapped in a non-abrasive barrier layer, then braced so it cannot shift inside the crate — movement, not impact, causes most transit damage. Corners and edges get additional protection, and the crate itself is built from ISPM 15 certified timber.

That certification is not optional. Untreated wood packaging is refused, quarantined, or destroyed at borders worldwide under the international phytosanitary standard. [What ISPM 15 means for your order](/blog/ispm-15-certification-what-it-means-for-your-order) explains the marking and why it exists.

Crates are built to be opened without power tools, and we include instructions. Do not attack a crate with a pry bar.

## Choosing a freight mode

**Sea freight** is the default for intercontinental shipments. It is dramatically cheaper for heavy goods and it is slow. For a single crate, transit plus port handling plus clearance is measured in weeks.

**Air freight** is faster and considerably more expensive — for a heavy crate the premium is substantial, since air freight prices on both weight and volume.

**Road freight** covers Europe and North Africa and is often the best combination of speed and cost within that range.

We quote the options for your destination at the time of order.

## Customs and duty

This is where most surprises happen, so it is worth being blunt.

You are the importer of record. Your country assesses duty, VAT, or sales tax on the declared value, and those charges are payable by you before the goods are released. Rates vary enormously by country and by tariff classification.

We provide a commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin, and we declare accurately. We will not under-declare value — it is fraud, it invalidates insurance, and if discovered it results in seizure and penalties for you as importer.

Check your own country's rate before you commit to a commission. On a piece in the upper budget range, duty can be a meaningful additional sum, and it is far better known in advance.

## Delivery

Standard freight delivers kerbside. The crate comes off the truck; getting it into your home is your responsibility.

For most clients we recommend arranging a white-glove or two-person delivery service at destination, which brings the piece inside, unpacks it, removes the crate, and positions it. A crated dining table is genuinely too heavy and awkward for one person, and a dropped table is not repairable.

Measure your access route before delivery day. Doorways, stair turns, lift dimensions, and tight corridors have defeated more furniture deliveries than freight damage has. Send us the narrowest point on your route and we will tell you whether the crate will pass.

## On arrival

Before you sign anything:

1. **Photograph the crate on the truck**, before it is moved.
2. **Inspect all six faces** for punctures, crushing, or water staining.
3. **Note any damage on the delivery paperwork** before signing. A clean signature is an acknowledgement that you received the goods in good condition.
4. **Unpack promptly** and photograph the piece from all angles.
5. **Contact us immediately** if anything is wrong.

Insurance claims depend almost entirely on documentation created in the first hour.

## Acclimatisation

Let the table sit in its room for a few days before heavy use. Wood responds to changes in humidity, and a piece that has crossed climates needs time to settle.

Do not position it against a radiator, over underfloor heating, or in sustained direct sunlight. [How to care for a resin river table](/blog/how-to-care-for-a-resin-river-table) covers placement and long-term maintenance.

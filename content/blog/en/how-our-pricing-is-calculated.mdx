Most studios quote custom furniture as a single opaque number. We would rather show the arithmetic, because when you understand what drives the cost you can make informed trade-offs instead of guessing which request is the expensive one.

## The five components

Every quote is built from the same five parts.

### 1. The slab

This is usually the largest line and the one buyers least expect. Slab price is driven by width above all else. A tree wide enough to yield a 110cm single-board top is substantially rarer than one yielding 85cm, and the price reflects scarcity rather than volume.

Length, thickness, species, and figure then modify it. Heavily figured wood — burl, crotch, curl — commands a premium because it is unpredictable and yields poorly.

For a dining table, expect the slab to represent roughly a third to a half of the total.

### 2. Resin

Resin is bought by weight and used by volume. The volume you need is a function of river width, top thickness, and how much of the void the timber leaves open.

This is where thickness compounds. A 6cm top does not use 20% more resin than a 5cm top for the same river — it uses 20% more across the entire pour, and deep pours must be layered to control the heat of the reaction, which adds days.

Pigments, metallics, and multi-layer effects add both material and labour.

### 3. Bench hours

The work that does not show up in photographs:

- Drying and moisture testing
- Flattening the raw slab
- Filling, stabilising, and sealing before the pour
- Building and sealing the pour form
- Post-cure flattening
- Progressive hand sanding, typically through many grits
- Final finish application in multiple coats

A flat clear pour is efficient. [Sculpted and textured surfaces](/blog/sculpted-and-textured-resin-surfaces) are not — they are shaped by hand after cure and can double finishing time.

### 4. The base

Steel bases are fabricated and finished to order. A simple flat bar or box-section base is modest; a sculptural or cantilevered base is an engineering exercise with its own cost.

### 5. Crating and freight

Crating uses ISPM 15 certified timber and is built around the specific piece. Freight then varies by weight, dimensions, destination, and service level. A table to Casablanca and the same table to Sydney are not comparable.

Import duty and local taxes are assessed by your country and are payable by you as importer. We declare accurately; we cannot control the rate. [International shipping and handling](/blog/international-shipping-and-handling) goes into this in detail.

## Where the money actually goes

If you want to reduce cost, the effective levers in order of impact are:

1. **Narrow the top.** Going from 110cm to 90cm can change the slab tier entirely.
2. **Reduce thickness.** Less resin, faster cure, fewer bench hours.
3. **Choose a clear or single-pigment pour** rather than layered effects.
4. **Accept a joined top** instead of a single board, where the design allows.
5. **Simplify the base.**

Levers that barely move the number: changing pigment colour, minor length changes, choosing between two species in the same tier.

## What we will not do

We do not quote low and revise upward once the slab is on the bench. If a board turns out to need more stabilising than expected, that is our risk, not a change order. The fixed quote is the point.

We also will not recommend a slab we think is wrong for your dimensions in order to hit a budget. If your budget and your brief do not meet, we will say so and suggest what to change.
